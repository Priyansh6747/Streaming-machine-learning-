    Gap Analysis — The Delay Tax¶

    Written after logging the index and per-paper notes. Maps every entry to the four lemmas, confirms/revises the "active but fragmented" claim, flags scoop risk, and recommends the baseline to reproduce.

    1. Is the field "active but fragmented, no accepted benchmark"? — CONFIRMED, with one update¶

    The claim in idea/scope.md holds, but must be sharpened in light of 2026 work:

    • Active: Gomes et al. 2022 (CSUR survey), Alencar et al. 2026 (DSP experimental survey), Mello et al. 2021/2022 (verification latency in active learning), Botacin & Gomes 2024 (COSE label-delay measurement), plus the adjacent hybrid line (STUDD 2023; Gamma-Weibull 2026).
    • Fragmented: confirmed. Terminology still split (verification latency, delayed labelling, label latency, extreme verification latency, delayed partially labelled streams). No canonical formulation, benchmark, or protocol — the Gomes survey's unified setting is a proposal, not yet the standard.
    • UPDATE (important): the immediate-label side of the field has now gained a proper benchmarking framework — Cerqueira et al., KDD'26 ("A Framework for Evaluating and Benchmarking Concept Drift Detection Methods"). So "no accepted benchmark" is no longer true for the immediate-label detector benchmark; it is still true for the delayed/partial-label detector benchmark. Our gap statement must be reworded to: "there is now a standard benchmark for immediate-label drift detection, but no equivalent under delayed, partial, or selective labels."

    Revised one-line gap: the field lacks (a) a detector-level benchmark under label-observation regimes, (b) formal bounds connecting label latency/coverage to detection quality, and (c) a standard confirmation protocol for early-warning + delayed-confirmation hybrids.

    2. Lemma-by-lemma coverage in the literature¶

    Lemma 1 — Reaction floor (detection cannot precede label arrival)¶

    • Partial treatment exists, empirically:
    • Gomes et al. 2022: AGRAWAL experiment — 10,000-instance delay shifts detections by ~10,000 instances; prequential accuracy dips below 50%.
    • Botacin & Gomes 2024: delay epochs map ~linearly to exposure increase; 81 epochs delay → +50% exposure; delay beyond that fully nullifies drift mitigation.
    • Joulani et al. 2013 (regret side): delay hurts learning multiplicatively (adversarial) / additively (stochastic) — the learning analogue of the floor.
    • No formal stopping-time bound (T ≥ τ + L_τ pointwise) exists in this literature. The classical quickest-detection-with-delayed-observations framing (Page 1954 / Lorden 1971 / Shiryaev) is the mathematical root, but no one has stated the reaction floor as a theorem for error-stream detectors under verification latency.
    • Verdict: the theorem is novel as stated (trivial to prove, novel to publish); the empirical phenomenon is already known — Ch3 must present the lemma as formalizing known behavior, and cite Gomes 2022 + Botacin 2024 as empirical corroboration.

    Lemma 2 — Power loss (1/√p sensitivity degradation under partial labels)¶

    • Adjacent treatments:
    • Gomes et al. 2022 survey: SLT discussion — effective sample size = labelled count (the same intuition, not a detector-level law).
    • Alencar et al. 2026 (DSP): the experimental survey likely measures detector degradation as label availability decreases — must be read in full; if it reports a 1/√p-like curve for ADWIN/DDM, our contribution shrinks to formalizing the factor (still publishable, but the empirics are taken).
    • STUDD 2023: sensitivity analysis over l_access ∈ {1,10,25,50}% shows relative method ordering changes with label access — qualitative, not a law.
    • Verdict: the 1/√p closed-form bound for windowed error-stream detectors appears genuinely novel. High-priority action: read Alencar 2026 to confirm.

    Lemma 3 — False-alarm inflation under batch arrival (design effect 1+(b−1)ρ)¶

    • No direct treatment found. Nothing in the logged literature studies batch/ bursty label arrival injecting autocorrelation into the observed error stream and inflating detector false alarms. The clustered-data variance factor is classical statistics, but its application to drift detectors under batch-reconciled labels is open.
    • Botacin & Gomes 2024 model queue size → delay, not batch correlation → false alarms; closest conceptual neighbor but not the same mechanism.
    • Verdict: the most clearly novel lemma. Least prior risk.

    Lemma 4 — Identifiability ceiling (pure posterior drift invisible to label-free)¶

    • Already established. Žliobaitė 2010 (ICDMW) proves the detectability conditions; Gomes et al. 2022 reproduce the table; STUDD 2023 empirically demonstrates the limitation (cannot catch posterior drift, explicitly stated).
    • Verdict: NOT novel as a claim. The novelty in Ch3 must be the use of it: the hybrid confirmation protocol that makes it actionable (label-free early warning + delayed supervised confirmation is the information-maximal architecture). Present Lemma 4 as a formal restatement of Žliobaitė, credited properly.

    3. SCOOP WARNINGS — read these before finalizing novelty claims¶

    1. Alencar et al. 2026 (DSP 182:106325) — HIGH. An experimental survey of exactly our Ch3 setting (delayed + partially labeled streams, detector taxonomy, label-scarcity experiments). We have only verified it exists; we have NOT read its results. If it already contains delay-vs-latency curves and label-coverage-vs-false-alarm curves for ADWIN/DDM/KSWIN, part of our Ch3 "measure the degradation" chapter is scooped as measurement — our remaining novelty is then the formal lemmas (esp. 1–3) and the hybrid protocol. Action: read it in full immediately.
    2. Botacin & Gomes 2024 (COSE) — MEDIUM-HIGH. Already quantified label-delay cost on real malware pipelines + pseudo-label mitigation. Do not claim: "we quantify that delayed labels hurt." Do claim: detector-level isolation, formal bounds, confirmation protocol, synthetic ground-truth delay curves. Also do not claim a novel time-aware metric without contrasting with exposure/AUT.
    3. Gomes et al. 2022 (CSUR) — MEDIUM. The unified setting and the delayed-label AGRAWAL illustration cover our formal framing at a survey level. They explicitly flag "forget outdated labels after drift" and the under-explored intersection (iv) as open — cite them as naming our gap.
    4. Agu et al. 2026 (JKSU-CIS) — LOW-MEDIUM. Pseudo-label adaptation gated by a statistical detector is architecturally close to our hybrid, but it sidesteps delayed ground truth rather than measuring it. Distinguish clearly.
    5. Mello et al. 2021/2022 — LOW. Verification latency in active learning overlaps our selective-labeling regime; not detector isolation.

    Net position: the combination of (formal lemmas 1–3) + (detector-level delay/coverage/batch curves on synthetic ground truth) + (a confirmation protocol, evaluated against naive-delayed, label-free-only, and immediate-label controls) remains unclaimed. The biggest single risk is #1.

    4. Which published baseline to reproduce for Phase 0¶

    Recommendation: Cerqueira et al., KDD'26 — "A Framework for Evaluating and Benchmarking Concept Drift Detection Methods."

    Why: - It is the most recent (2026), venue-strong (KDD, open access, CC-BY, code public), and directly relevant: reproducing one of its detector comparisons (e.g., ADWIN vs DDM vs KSWIN on one injected-drift real dataset) exercises exactly the harness + metrics Ch1 needs. - It is immediate-label; that's fine for Phase 0 (we need the control numbers first) and it lets us later extend it with delay regimes in Ch3, which is a clean narrative: "we take the field's new standard benchmark and break it with delayed labels." - Feasibility note: it uses MOA-family methods (Java) for some detectors; River and CapyMOA both implement ADWIN/DDM/EDDM/KSWIN/Page-Hinkley, so a River reproduction of a subset (2–3 detectors, 1–2 datasets, abrupt + gradual) is realistic.

    Fallback alternatives (if KDD'26 proves too heavy to reproduce): - Gonçalves et al. 2014 (ESWA) — the classic detector comparison; reproducing a headline number (e.g., no detector dominates on SEA) is cheap and citable. - Gomes et al. 2022 survey's AGRAWAL experiment — reproduce the delayed-label shift (~10k instances) as a sanity check that our harness correctly models delay; this doubles as a first Ch3 pilot result.

    Recommended Phase 0 checklist: 1. Pin River version; verify ADWIN/DDM/EDDM/KSWIN/PHT implementations exist. 2. Reproduce one KDD'26-style detector comparison on a real dataset with injected drift (or fallback: one ESWA-2014-style SEA comparison). 3. As a stretch: reproduce Gomes 2022's delayed-AGRAWAL shift as the first delayed-label pilot.

    6. Ch1 status update — direct streaming-vs-batch comparisons (added Sep 2026)¶

    After web research logged into index.md §1 (subgroup "Streaming vs batch / retraining-policy comparisons"), the landscape for the Ch1/Phase-1 thesis question ("take non-streaming models and beat them on real metrics") is now clear:

    Direct comparisons already exist — Ch1 is not novel as a paradigm comparison.

    • Read et al. 2012 (IDA): instance-incremental vs batch-incremental under drift — no universal winner.
    • Chen 2019 (SEAMS): retrained vs incremental on 1,360 conditions — winner depends on algorithm and drift intensity; incremental faster, often equal-or-better.
    • Amekoe et al. 2024: tuned frequent-retrain batch beats streaming under delay + rare class (already logged).
    • Applied 2024–2026 (Hans & Zühlke 2026 Water Supply; Abdoune et al. 2026 ESWA; Bartz-Beielstein & Hans 2024): online/incremental wins operationally under drift intensity and on runtime.

    Scope-defining new evidence — Dasari 2026 (arXiv:2608.19488):

    • With per-sample incremental updates, retraining policy is irrelevant (54 paired comparisons, no practical difference vs no-retrain).
    • Without incremental updates, periodic retraining beats both reactive (drift-triggered / error-threshold) policies under abrupt and gradual drift; reactive only wins under recurring drift.
    • ⇒ The thesis claim must be worded about streaming learners, not about drift detectors used as batch-refit triggers. "Event-driven beats clock-driven" is only true when the learner updates per sample; a detector firing at batch cadence inherits the clock's weaknesses. Our thesis.md §1/§3 mechanism (bounded recovery via per-sample updates) already reflects this; keep it explicit.

    What remains ours for Ch1 (defensible, narrow):

    1. The reactivity-metric family applied to this comparison — recovery time, damage area, rolling-Kappa dip depth, detection delay — none of the found studies measure these (they use mean accuracy/MAE and runtime).
    2. A fair, pre-registered protocol with frozen + scheduled + oracle-tuned baselines, ≥10 seeds, Friedman + Nemenyi, and an explicit win card (one claim per metric, dataset + number).
    3. Quantifying the scheduled-batch phase-noise effect (recovery time scatters uniformly over the retrain interval) rather than reporting one lucky schedule.
    4. The Delay Tax (Ch3) remains the project's main novelty; Ch1 is best framed as the rigorous baseline chapter the literature lacks (consistent with KDD'26 as the detector-side benchmark to build on).
