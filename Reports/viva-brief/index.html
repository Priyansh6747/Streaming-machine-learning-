    Viva Brief — Streaming ML / the Delay Tax¶

    Purpose: preparation for a progress viva — what is done, what is next, and why this is a Type 2b project. Every number here is traceable to a committed artifact.

    Date prepared: 2026-09-22 · Scope as executed: Phase 0 complete, Phase 1 started (Exp-01, Exp-02 concluded).

    This page is excluded from the published mkdocs site (exclude_docs). It lives in the vault for your own use and does not appear on GitHub Pages.

    0. If you remember only five things¶

    1. The claim: on a drifting stream, all models are equally good before the change — the difference is what happens after. A model that keeps learning heals itself; a model trained once and frozen does not.
    2. The evidence: 11 seeds. Frozen recovered 0/11. Streaming had a real recovery on 8/11; on the other three it never left its pre-drift band. The old “11/11 recovered” counted the metric floor as a recovery.
    3. The honest headline is not 294×. It is: frozen never recovers, and the streaming model pays ≈6.9× less corrected damage_ref on the mean (74.8 vs 515.0). The legacy 294× and the 24× worst-vs-best claim are retired; on the clean panel the worst streaming seed is 0.74× the best frozen seed. E01 is the authority (see §7.1).
    4. The project type is 2b. Development = the instrumented experiment pipeline (built) + the containerised continual-learning service (next). Say the second half out loud, unprompted.
    5. The Delay Tax — the headline contribution — is not yet run. Zero experiments. It is the roadmap, and you should present it as such rather than letting an examiner discover it.

    1. The 60-second pitch¶

    Almost all ML treats the dataset as a finished object. Streaming ML drops that: data arrives one sample at a time, forever, too large to store, and the model must learn incrementally in a single pass in bounded memory. Because the world generating the data keeps changing, the model must also notice when it becomes wrong and adapt — that is concept drift, and it is the project's problem.

    The project has three parts, in increasing novelty:

    1. Baseline (Ch1) — the rigorous comparison the field lacks: streaming vs non-streaming, on reactivity metrics rather than average accuracy.
    2. System (Ch2) — a real containerised continual-learning service, measuring the operational costs the literature never reports (adaptation latency, replica divergence, backpressure, restart cost).
    3. Contribution (Ch3) — the Delay Tax: production labels arrive late, partially, and selectively; the field assumes they arrive instantly and completely. Formalised as four lemmas plus a hybrid detector, and measured.

    The thesis mechanism in one line: batch models update on a clock, streaming models update on evidence. Streaming's recovery time is d + a (bounded, data-driven); a scheduled batch model's is r_next − τ (luck of phase); a frozen model's is undefined. Streaming wins whenever d + a < r_next − τ, and that ratio is the quantitative content of the project.

    2. Type compliance — why this is 2b, not a pure research project¶

    The rule: the Major Project may be 2a (full development) or 2b (research + development). Pure research with no development is not allowed.

    This is the first thing an examiner will test, so lead with it.

    2c. The honest risk, and the line to say¶

    The risk: to date the delivered artifacts are predominantly research + simulation. The development that exists is infrastructure around research (pipeline, packaging, docs, CI) — there is no deployed system yet, and main.py is still a stub.

    Say this, don't wait to be asked:

    "Chapter 1 is the measurement half and it is instrumented — code, artifact, figure, one command to reproduce. Chapter 2 is the development half and it is next: a containerised continual-learning service where the model that serves is the model that learns, with a live drift monitor, Prometheus/Grafana and a load generator. The delay-tax experiments are built on the same harness. That is the research-plus-development structure, and Chapter 2 is where the development weight sits."

    Then be concrete about the near-term development deliverable, because "planned" is weak and "built" is strong. If there is time before the viva, the single highest-value development artifact to have in hand is the config-driven harness — one runner that expresses Exp-01/02 as config rather than as a bespoke script (see §7.2).

    4. The numbers board — memorise these¶

    4a. Setup (identical in both experiments)¶
    Stream SEA synthetic, one continuous U(0,10)³ draw, binary labels
    Length 60,000 samples · warm-up 12,000 (20%) · drift at τ = 30,000
    Drift abrupt: rule changes from x₀+x₁ > 8 to x₀+x₁ > 9
    Noise 0.1 (10% label noise)
    Evaluation prequential (test-then-train), post-warm-up n = 48,000
    Rolling metric Cohen Kappa, window w = 1,000
    Recovery rule κ ≥ 0.95·κ₀ for 500 consecutive samples, within 5,000 samples of τ
    Streaming model DeepRiver RollingClassifier, RNN 3→16→2, window 500, Adam, lr 1e-3
    Frozen model RandomForestClassifier(100), fit once on the 12,000-sample warm-up

    4e. The four lemmas (Ch3 — the theory, none of it yet empirically tested)¶

    # Lemma Statement Novelty
    L1 Reaction floor T ≥ τ + L_τ pointwise — no error-stream detector can alarm before the drift's labels arrive Novel as stated; phenomenon known empirically
    L2 Power loss Sensitivity degrades by 1/√p; Δ_min ∝ 1/√(pW) Appears genuinely novel — check Alencar 2026 first
    L3 False-alarm inflation Batch arrival inflates error-rate variance by 1 + (b−1)ρ Most clearly novel — no prior treatment found
    L4 Identifiability ceiling Pure posterior drift is invisible to any label-free detector (KL = 0) Not novel — Žliobaitė 2010. Ours is only the use (hybrid motivation)

    Scoop risk to acknowledge proactively: Alencar et al. 2026 (DSP) is an experimental survey of exactly the Ch3 setting; it must be read in full before any novelty claim. Botacin & Gomes 2024 already quantified label-delay cost on a real pipeline — so never claim "we show delayed labels hurt".

    6. Attacks, ranked, with pre-empted answers¶

    Attack 1 — "A frozen model never recovering is trivially true. You built a strawman."¶

    Correct, and lead with it. A model with no update mechanism cannot recover by construction — that was the point of Exp-01 as the weakest form of the claim, and it is documented as such. The informative comparison is a scheduled-retrain batch model, and Exp-03 is exactly that: streaming recovery d + a against the batch model's r_next − τ, plus the pre-registered prediction that the batch recovery time scatters uniformly over [0, k] as pure phase noise.

    Attack 2 — "Your headline 294× damage gap is inflated." ← resolved, but know the history¶

    Correct — and E01 already fixed it. The legacy damage_area integrated from τ to the recovery point; on 8/11 seeds recovery fired at τ, truncating the streaming integral. The 294× and 24× are upper bounds and are retired. E01 reran the pilot with a fixed-window, panel-shared damage_ref:

    • damage_ref: 74.8 ± 61.8 vs 515.0 ± 97.1 samples·κ → ≈6.9×
    • damage_full: 321.4 ± 203.8 vs 3,472.9 ± 559.7 → ≈10.8×
    • worst-vs-best: 0.74×, not 24×
    • frozen recovered 0/11 — unchanged

    The corrected claim is smaller and defensible. Do not quote 294× or 24× except as a retired upper bound. See §7.1 and E01-metric-v2.md.

    Attack 3 — "There is no drift detector anywhere in your detector project."¶

    Was fair; now partly addressed. E01/02 measured recovery from the rolling window alone. E04 (2026-11-12, first pass) benchmarks eight detectors — ADWIN, KSWIN, PageHinkley, DDM, EDDM, FHDDM, HDDM-A, HDDM-W — across four generators, four drift shapes, two hosts, raw and smoothed mistake streams. E17 (2026-11-13) replays the same eight on real Elec2 records with an injected label swap. HDDM-A on the raw signal is the best default on the static host (balanced score 0.93/0.96/0.96/0.79 for abrupt/gradual/incremental/recurring), but on the adaptive HAT host ADWIN wins abrupt (1.00 vs 0.40) and recurring (0.77 vs 0.59); the best-host mean is ADWIN 0.935 vs HDDM-A 0.910. So the default is host-conditional, not universal. Recurring remains the hard case; smoothing raises false alarms. Both are 5-seed first passes, not the full 11-seed tuned matrix. Say that, not “we benchmarked all detectors”. The design response still stands: per-sample incremental updates make the retraining policy less important (Dasari 2026, 54 paired comparisons). See E04-detector-map.md and E17-real-detector-map.md.

    Attack 4 — "Your own scope says no deep learning." ← known inconsistency¶

    idea/scope.md §9 lists "❌ Deep learning / deep-river" as out of scope, yet DeepRiver is the subject of both concluded experiments. The Exp-01 write-up acknowledges this as "a deliberate, teacher-relevant extension". Fix it properly before the viva (§7.2) — an undocumented scope reversal reads as drift, which is unfortunate for a concept-drift project.

    Attack 5 — "11 seeds on one stream is not Demšar."¶

    Correct. The pre-registered bar was ≥10 seeds and it is met, but Friedman + Nemenyi requires many datasets × many learners. What exists is a within-stream distribution; cross-dataset significance is Exp-04/05. Say "distribution", not "statistical significance".

    Attack 6 — "The metric definitions are arbitrary." (0.95 band, 500 stretch, 5,000 lookahead)¶

    They were pre-registered before the run with an explicit falsification budget — that is the defence, and it is a good one. The gap is a missing sensitivity analysis over those constants: admit it as a known next step.

    Attack 7 — "You promised the Delay Tax and ran none of it."¶

    True — zero experiments on the headline contribution. Frame it as sequencing, not slippage: Ch1 is the control condition everything is measured against, and it goes first by design. The lemmas are cheap to verify empirically, and L1's experiment is a delay sweep on the harness that already exists.

    Attack 8 — "Your streaming model is slower than the batch one."¶

    True and reported. 484 samples/s on an RTX 3050 for a 2.3k-parameter net (44/s on CPU) versus 0.17 s to fit the RF. The cost column stands unfavourable and is not hidden; Amekoe 2024 (ARF 2.2× slower) shows this is a field-wide tension, not an implementation accident.

    Attack 9 — "H2 was refuted, then restored. Which is it?"¶

    Not a flip-flop — one seed versus eleven. On seed 42 the two were level (0.755 vs 0.759), so H2 was recorded as refuted; on 11 seeds the frozen model leads (0.767 vs 0.751) and H2 is supported. The honest reading: the frozen model wins the stationary phase slightly and still loses the run because it collapses permanently at τ. The n=1 estimate was noise, which is exactly why Exp-02 exists.

    Attack 10 — "Where is the development? This looks like research only."¶

    §2. Have the development inventory ready, name Chapter 2 concretely, and be honest that the system is not built yet.

    7. What changed, and what remains¶

    7.1 The damage-area defect — resolved by E01 (2026-11-12)¶

    The defect (history). The legacy damage_area integrated from τ to the recovery point. On 8/11 seeds the recovery rule collapsed to τ (the model never left its band), so the streaming integral covered a single sample while frozen — which never recovered — integrated the full window. Both 294× and 24× were upper bounds.

    The fix (E01). Fixed 5,000-sample window; κ_ref shared across the model panel; recovery only counted after a real band exit. The old artifacts are kept; E01 is a new artifact.

    Corrected result (11 seeds):

    Metric DeepRiver Frozen RF
    damage_ref (primary) 74.8 ± 61.8 (worst 217.9) 515.0 ± 97.1 (best 295.2)
    damage_full 321.4 ± 203.8 3,472.9 ± 559.7
    recovery_rate 8/11 0/11
    Worst-vs-best 0.74× —

    What to say:

    1. 294× and 24× are retired. Corrected mean gap: ≈6.9× (damage_ref) or ≈10.8× (damage_full). On the clean panel the worst streaming seed is 0.74× the best frozen seed, not 24×.
    2. Frozen never recovers (0/11) — unchanged and still the strongest claim.
    3. Streaming had a real recovery on 8/11, not 11/11; the other three never left their band. “Recovery = 0” is the metric floor, not instant recovery.
    4. AUE is secondary. The timing oracle knows when the drift happened but is refit on pre-drift data; its damage_ref (505) is almost frozen. AUE measures excess over a weak oracle, not distance from an upper bound.
    5. Panel composition. The artifact is the clean DeepRiver + frozen + oracle panel. The pre-fix four-model artifact is archived at results/_archive/e01_5model/; the numbers above supersede it.

    What survives untouched: frozen recovered on 0/11; DeepRiver's damage_ref was lower on 11/11 seeds; full-run accuracy/Kappa are 0.883/0.753 vs 0.854/0.685. The corrected claim is smaller and defensible.

    7.2 Close the two consistency gaps¶

    • Amend the scope properly. Add a dated note to idea/scope.md §9 recording that deep learners were deliberately brought in for the Ch1 pilot, with the reason. Also consider adding a classical arm (HAT / ARF) to Exp-03 so the thesis claim is not deep-net-specific.
    • Build the reusable harness. Today exp01 and exp02 duplicate drift_metrics "verbatim" by hand. A config-driven runner that expresses both as configuration is the Ch1 exit criterion and the single most convincing piece of 2b development you could have in hand for this viva. It also makes the metric fix a config change.

    8. Likely questions — short answers¶

    Question Answer
    What is concept drift? P_t(X,y) ≠ P_{t+Δ}(X,y) — the joint distribution changes over time. Real drift changes P(y|X); virtual drift changes only P(X).
    Why is prequential evaluation right? Its the accepted protocol for ordered streams (Gama 2013). Random splits leak future information; test-then-train scores each sample before it trains.
    Why Kappa and not accuracy? Chance-corrected and robust to class imbalance. Accuracy alone overstates learning when temporal persistence creates a strong no-change baseline.
    Why SEA? Synthetic streams give ground-truth drift positions — without a known τ, detection delay and recovery time are uncomputable.
    Why a rolling window of 500? It is the bounded-memory mechanism: old samples fall out, which is what makes the model drift-adaptive. It was a pre-registered default, not fitted.
    What is damage_ref? The skill lost while the model is wrong, integrated over a fixed drift window against a panel-shared κ_ref, in samples·κ. Current headline: 74.8 vs 515.0 samples·κ (≈6.9×). The legacy "damage area" is retired (§7.1).
    What would falsify the thesis? Streaming recovery comparable to a sane retrain interval (d + a ≥ r_next − τ); no dip at τ (dataset problem); no significant difference under Friedman/Nemenyi; reactivity win not worth the resource cost.
    Why is this not just a tutorial? The gap is specific and open: a standard benchmark now exists for immediate-label drift detection (KDD'26), but none for delayed/partial/selective labels, and no formal bounds connect label latency to detection quality.
    What is the single novel claim? The confirmation protocol — label-free early warning + delayed supervised confirmation. L4 proves label-free cannot confirm; L1 proves supervised cannot be early; so the hybrid is the information-maximal architecture.
    What if the hybrid fails? A complete negative result: "we quantified how delayed labels break existing methods, and why the obvious fix is insufficient." Lemmas 1–3 hold regardless. Ch3 cannot fail — which is why it is the bet.
    Why is it 2b and not 2a? It is both halves: measurement (Ch1/Ch3) and a deployed system (Ch2) — plus the instrumented pipeline already built. It is explicitly not research-only.
    What is the biggest threat to the project? Scope creep, not difficulty. The scope guards exist for that reason, and every chapter has a survivable failure mode.
