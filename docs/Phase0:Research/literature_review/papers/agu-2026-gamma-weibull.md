# Statistical Drift Detection with Parametric Gamma–Weibull and Adaptive Deep Learning for Malware Detection in IoT Data Streams

**Full citation:** Agu, F. I., Andok, R., Klarák, J., & Dora, J. R. (2026). Statistical drift detection with parametric Gamma–Weibull and adaptive deep learning for malware detection in IoT data streams. *Journal of King Saud University — Computer and Information Sciences*, 38, Article 673. https://doi.org/10.1007/s44443-026-01091-w

**Status:** ✅ Verified (full text read from Material/)

---

## Why this paper matters

A **2026 hybrid drift-detection + adaptation framework** that is the closest
recent work to our Ch3 architecture in spirit: a statistical detector gates model
adaptation, and adaptation uses auto-generated labels (pseudo-labels + clustering)
rather than human labels. It is a strong "adjacent hybrid" citation and a useful
contrast case.

## Method

- **Detector:** Gamma–Weibull log-likelihood-ratio **CUSUM**. Pre-change modeled
  as Gamma(α₀, β₀), post-change as Weibull(k₁, λ₁); LLR per observation
  `Λ(X_t) = log(f₁(X_t)/f₀(X_t))`; CUSUM recursion `S_t = max(0, S_{t−1} + Λ(X_t))`;
  alarm when `S_t ≥ h`.
- **Threshold calibration:** ARL₀ (in-control average run length) target 2000,
  with approximation `ARL₀ ≈ (e^h − 1)/|μ₀|`, so `h = ln(1 + ARL₀|μ̂₀|)`.
- **Expected delay:** `E[D] ≈ h/μ₁` where μ₁ is expected LLR under post-change.
- **Adaptation:** on alarm, buffer B of b=300 post-drift sequences; labels
  generated by confidence-threshold pseudo-labeling (τ=0.80) with K-means
  clustering for low-confidence samples; only the 66-parameter classifier head of
  an LSTM/CNN is fine-tuned (backbone frozen).

- MC=30 Wilcoxon signed-rank: all adaptive-vs-baseline p < 5.7e-5.
- Runtime (CPU): full pipeline 1.43 s; monitoring <150 ms; pseudo-labeling 2.4 ms;
  model+adaptation memory <50 KB (edge-deployable claim).
- Real data: IoT-23 benign (n=451) and malware (n=14,784) duration streams; first

- Honest limitation: Lognormal fits the data better than Gamma/Weibull; the pair
  is chosen for closed-form tractability, not best fit.

## How it differs from Ch3 / our lemmas

- **Different signal.** They detect distributional drift in the *input feature*
  (connection duration) via parametric LLR, not drift in a classifier's error
  stream. Our detectors (ADWIN/DDM/EDDM on errors) are the classical supervised
  setting; the two are complementary but not directly comparable.
- **No delayed ground truth.** Their "labels" are pseudo-labels generated by the
  model itself — the delay problem is sidestepped, not measured. They do not
  quantify detection delay vs label latency, power loss vs label rate, or
  batch-arrival effects.
- **No formal lemmas.** The ARL₀ approximation and E[D]≈h/μ₁ are classical CUSUM
  results, not new bounds about label delay.
- Their detector-gated adaptation is a useful design contrast: adaptation only on
  statistically significant alarms (avoids unnecessary retraining) — aligns with
  our hybrid's "confirm before acting" philosophy.

## Recommended use

- Cite as 2026 evidence that "statistical detector + auto-labeled adaptation" is
  an active, viable pattern (and to show we are aware of it).
- Their ARL₀ calibration discussion is a good reference for detector
  threshold-setting in Ch1.
- Explicitly distinguish: they remove the label problem via pseudo-labels; we
  *measure* the label problem and confirm with delayed ground truth.
