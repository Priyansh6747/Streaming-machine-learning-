    DriftDeck — drift monitoring that keeps working when your labels are late¶

    Product doc — turning the Delay Tax research into a product.

    One line: DriftDeck is an ML observability platform that detects and prices model drift in production — and keeps doing so when the truth arrives late, partially, or selectively. Its engine is a hybrid detector (label-free early warning + delayed supervised confirmation), and its headline number is the drift cost in business terms, not an abstract accuracy drop.

    (Working name — the file and title rename in one edit if you want a different one.)

    1. TL;DR¶
    What ML drift monitoring for production models whose labels are delayed, partial, or selective
    Who MLOps / ML platform teams running fraud, lending, ads, intrusion, or content models
    The gap Every drift tool assumes labels arrive with the prediction. In production they don't (chargebacks take 30–90 days).
    The wedge DriftDeck is delayed-label-native — it models the delay, not papers over it
    The engine Hybrid detector = label-free early warning + delayed supervised confirmation
    The metric Damage area — skill lost × time wrong, converted to money
    The moat Four formal lemmas (the Delay Tax) that let the product state honest limits, not marketing promises
    The build order Validate the lemmas → MVP on fraud → hosted

    2. The problem¶

    A model is deployed. The world changes. The model silently gets worse. Two facts make this expensive, and they compound:

    Fact 1 — drift is invisible to the people who own the model. You can't see accuracy in production without labels, and accuracy is exactly what's degrading. By the time a quarterly report catches it, the loss has already happened.

    Fact 2 — the labels that would reveal drift arrive late, or never. This is the Delay Tax, and it is measured:

    Cost of late labels Number Source
    Delayed labels raise user exposure +50% at 81 (DDM) / 61 (EDDM) epochs of delay Botacin & Gomes 2024
    Delayed labels cut detection up to −75% Botacin & Gomes 2024
    A 10,000-instance label delay shifts drift detection by ~10,000 instances (prequential accuracy pinned below 50%) Gomes et al. 2022
    Batch retraining starves without labels tuned batch wins under delay+rare-class (AUCPR 23.41 vs 14.23) Amekoe et al. 2024

    The production stories are concrete: a fraud model approves a transaction now and learns the truth when the chargeback lands in 30–90 days. An ad model serves now and learns the conversion next week. An intrusion model flags now and gets the analyst verdict later, or never. Labels arrive in overnight batches, out of order, and only for flagged cases — which makes the observed errors a biased sample of true errors.

    Why this is a product and not a feature: the market's existing tools solve "detect drift when labels are here." The harder problem — "keep watching when labels are not here, and be honest about what you can and can't know yet" — is unsolved. That is the product.

    3. The wedge: what everyone else assumes away¶

    The research's gap analysis (26-page review, 38 papers, notes.pdf) established the wedge precisely. The delayed/partial-label literature is fragmented (five names for one thing: verification latency, delayed labelling, label latency, extreme verification latency, delayed partially labelled streams) and unbenchmarked: a standard benchmark now exists for immediate-label drift detection (Cerqueira et al., KDD'26), but none exists for delayed, partial, or selective labels.

    Concretely, the competition falls into two camps:

    Camp Examples What they assume The gap DriftDeck fills
    Data/target drift monitors Evidently, Arize, Fiddler, WhyLabs Labels eventually available, or ignore the delay Don't model when the truth arrives
    Label-free performance estimators NannyML (CBPE), Seldon Alibi Detect Estimate accuracy without labels Great — but they treat "no labels yet" as a state, not a costed, bounded timeline
    DriftDeck — Labels are a stochastic observation process with latency Models the delay, gives the earliest provable answer, prices the wait

    The differentiation is narrow but real, and it is exactly what the four lemmas give.

    4. From research to product — the mapping¶

    Every piece of the research becomes a product feature. This table is the conversion.

    Research artifact Product feature
    Lemma 1 — reaction floor (T ≥ τ + L_τ) "Earliest you can know" — the product tells the customer, honestly, the fastest possible confirmation given their median label latency, and shows the early warning meanwhile
    Lemma 2 — power loss (1/√p) Coverage dial — "you label 10% of traffic, so my confidence is ~3.2× noisier" — a per-model sensitivity/confidence number
    Lemma 3 — batch-arrival inflation (1+(b−1)ρ) False-alarm suppression on batch-reconciled labels (overnight jobs) — fewer spurious pages
    Lemma 4 — identifiability ceiling Honest limits — "this drift type is invisible without labels; here's what I can and can't see"
    Hybrid detector (Ch3) The core engine — early warning (fast, may be false) + delayed confirmation (slow, reliable)
    Damage area / recovery time / dip depth (Exp-01/02) The money metric — drift priced as Σ (baseline − actual) × cost-per-error
    Prequential harness (Ch1) The backtest/trust engine — prove the detector on your data before you deploy it
    The system chapter (Ch2) The deployment/scale story — replica divergence, backpressure, restart cost
    Exp-01/02 (measured) The proof-of-concept demo — a model that keeps learning heals itself; a frozen one doesn't

    5. The engine — the hybrid detector¶

    The product's core. It is justified by construction, not by tuning:

    • Stage 1 — label-free early warning (on P(X), live): KS / MMD / Jensen–Shannon two-sample tests over a sliding window. Fast, catches drift immediately, but also fires on harmless virtual drift.
    • Stage 2 — supervised confirmation (on the delayed error stream): join the late labels to the buffered predictions, form the error stream, run ADWIN / DDM / KSWIN. Reliable, but bounded below by the reaction floor (can't fire before labels land).
    • The hybrid logic: a warning inside a tolerance window, then a confirmation → alarm. A warning that never confirms → revoked, with its false-alarm cost recorded.

    Why this is the information-maximal architecture: Lemma 4 proves Stage 1 cannot confirm (pure posterior drift is invisible to P(X)), and Lemma 1 proves Stage 2 cannot be early. So the hybrid is the only design that is both early and reliable given the available information. That is a defensible engineering claim, not a slogan.

    Cheap to run: the detectors are O(1) per sample — literature puts monitoring at <150 ms and <50 KB of state per detector (Agu et al. 2026). DriftDeck can watch thousands of models on one node.

    6. The money metric — damage area → drift cost¶

    Recovery time tells you how long the model was wrong. Damage area tells you how much skill × time you lost:

    damage = Σ (κ₀ − κ(t))   for t = drift → recovery      # in samples · Kappa
drift_cost = damage × cost_per_error(currency)        # the number the business sees

    This is the product's "so what." A PM doesn't care about Cohen's Kappa. They care that "the model was 0.173 κ below baseline for 12,000 transactions, costing ~$X." DriftDeck reports the dollar number, with the Kappa behind it.

    (Honest note from the research: the damage-area metric itself has a truncation defect — see ML.md §11.1 — that must be fixed before it ships. It is a known, bounded issue, not a mystery.)

    7. Positioning vs competitors¶

    Tool Drift detection Label delay handled Prices drift in money Honest limits
    Evidently ✔ ✖ (assumes labels) partial ✖
    Arize / Fiddler / WhyLabs ✔ ✖ partial ✖
    NannyML ✔ (CBPE, label-free) ✔ implicitly partial ✖
    Seldon Alibi Detect ✔ ✖ ✖ ✖
    DriftDeck ✔ hybrid ✔ native ✔ headline ✔ lemmas

    The honest position: DriftDeck does not win "who has the most charts." It wins the one narrow, expensive case — "my labels are late and I need to know how wrong I am right now, and what it's costing me."

    8. Who it's for — and the killer demo¶

    Ideal customer profile: a team with production ML models where ground truth is intrinsically delayed or partial — fraud/payments (chargebacks 30–90 days), lending (defaults), ad conversion (weeks), cybersecurity (analyst triage), insurance (claims).

    The killer demo is fraud, because it is the canonical Delay Tax story and it is visible: a fraud model approved a transaction; the chargeback lands 45 days later. A fraud team already understands the lag. Show them, on their own stream, that DriftDeck (a) warned within hours using input drift, (b) confirmed when the chargebacks landed, (c) priced the accumulated loss — and that their current monitoring said nothing for 45 days. That demo closes.

    9. Architecture¶

    The product is an observability sidecar/layer, not a serving framework — it attaches to any model (batch or streaming, yours or theirs). This is deliberately the Ch2 "systems" DNA: the hard part is not the math, it is the two-stream join and the operational reality.

    Components¶

    Component Responsibility Notes
    driftdeck-sdk Python SDK wrapping predict(); emits (id, features, ŷ); also a client to submit late labels Drop-in for sklearn/River/PyTorch serving
    Event backbone Kafka/Redpanda (scale) or Redis Streams (single-node) Two topics: inference, labels. The delay lives in the label topic
    Label-join + buffer Joins late labels to predictions; retains predictions ≥ the max label delay The two-stream join is the hard engineering, not the math
    Stage-1 detector Label-free early warning on P(X) KS/MMD/Jensen–Shannon windows
    Stage-2 detector Supervised confirmation on the delayed error stream ADWIN/DDM/EDDM/KSWIN/Page-Hinkley
    Hybrid logic Correlates warnings and confirmations; alarms, revokes, records false-alarm cost Encodes Lemmas 1 & 4
    Damage estimator Rolling κ, dip depth, recovery time, damage area → $ Configurable cost_per_error
    Store Prometheus (alerts) + ClickHouse/Timescale (per-model drift history, high cardinality) Two-tier: real-time vs analytic
    UI — "the deck" Live drift timeline, damage in $, lemma bounds, per-model health The demo dashboard
    Alerts Webhooks, PagerDuty, Slack; a retrain/rollback trigger (CI or model-registry webhook) Closes the loop from detect to act
    Backtest harness Offline prequential replay of the customer's own stream The trust engine + sales demo

    The response half (optional, and where the research's adaptation side ships)¶

    Detection is the core. But the research also measured recovery (Exp-01/02: a model that keeps learning heals itself; a frozen one doesn't). So DriftDeck ships a "respond" tier: on a confirmed drift, trigger retrain / rollback / model-registry swap — or, for customers who want it, serve a continually-learning model that self-adapts within a bounded window. That second tier is what Exp-01/02 actually demonstrated.

    10. Deployment model¶

    Tier Shape When
    MVP docker-compose, single node, Redis Streams validate + first demo
    Scale k8s + Helm, Kafka/Redpanda, horizontal detector workers multi-model, high QPS
    On-prem / edge single binary or sidecar per model fraud/security customers with data-residency constraints

    Detector cost is the enabler: O(1) per sample, <50 KB state per detector, so a small fleet of detector workers watches a large number of models. The expensive part is the label-join retention buffer — sized by the customer's max label delay, not by traffic.

    11. The build order (roadmap)¶

    Ordered so the product de-risks itself and the academic claims de-risk themselves — they are now the same thing.

    Phase Build Exit criterion De-risks
    A — Validate Run the delayed-label experiments (the project's Exp-05): the four lemmas, on synthetic ground truth The 1/√p, reaction-floor, and batch-inflation curves hold empirically The core engine is true, not assumed — and Alencar 2026 is read
    B — MVP SDK + detector core + label-join + damage estimator + minimal UI, on one real dataset (CreditCard fraud) End-to-end: a live fraud stream, a late chargeback, a confirmed drift, a dollar figure The killer demo
    C — Harden Alerts, retrain trigger, model-registry integrations, auth/tenancy, multi-model A pilot customer runs it in shadow mode Product–market fit signal
    D — Distribute Hosted SaaS + on-prem enterprise, OSS the SDK + detectors Paying customer Business

    The research's own roadmap (Exp-03 baseline ladder, Exp-04 real streams, Exp-05 delay tax) is Phase A–B of the product. There is no wasted work — the academic experiments become the product's proof and its backtest engine.

    12. Business model (brief)¶

    • OSS core — SDK + detectors + the hybrid logic. Builds trust and distribution in the MLOps community, and it is cheap to give away (the detectors are the research).
    • Paid — hosted (multi-model, retention, alerting, SSO, compliance, support) and on-prem. Per-model or per-event pricing, not per-seat — the value scales with drift cost, not headcount.
    • The wedge in pricing: the dollar "drift cost" the product reports is the ROI number that justifies the subscription. "You lost $X to drift last quarter; DriftDeck costs $Y."

    13. Risks — honest¶

    Risk Severity Mitigation
    Crowded space (Evidently, NannyML, Arize, Fiddler, WhyLabs, Seldon) High Differentiation is narrow-but-real (delay); go deep on fraud/security, not wide
    NannyML already does label-free performance estimation Medium-High Differentiate on the timeline (earliest provable answer) + the dollar metric + honest limits, not on "we also estimate accuracy"
    Lemmas are unvalidated — the core claim is unproven until Exp-05 High Phase A runs first; the lemmas are cheap to test and can degrade to empirical claims if a proof resists
    Alencar 2026 (DSP) may scoop the measurement Medium-High Read it now; if it already has delay-vs-latency curves, the novelty shifts to the formal bounds + the hybrid protocol + the product
    Thin proof-of-concept (one synthetic stream, no detector actually run) Medium Position Exp-01/02 as the mechanism demo; the product's proof is Phase A–B, not the two experiments
    Buyer is MLOps, not infra Medium The author's infra background helps the build, not the sale — needs an MLOps-minded co-founder/early customer
    Data residency / compliance (fraud, security) Medium On-prem/edge tier first-class, not an afterthought

    14. What the research already gives you¶

    This is not starting from zero. The project hands the product:

    1. A pre-registered, reproducible proof of the mechanism — Exp-01/02: a model that keeps learning recovers from drift in bounded time; a frozen one never does (0/11 seeds). That is the "adaptation" half of the story, measured, with artifacts.
    2. The metric family — damage area, recovery time, dip depth — the product's money metric is already defined and (partially) measured.
    3. The four lemmas — a genuine, citable theory that becomes the "honest limits" feature and the marketing moat.
    4. The hybrid detector design — justified by construction, ready to build.
    5. The prequential harness — the future backtest/trust engine.
    6. The systems knowledge — replica divergence, backpressure, restart cost — the deployment/scale story no ML competitor has.

    The honest gap: the lemmas and the hybrid are specified but not yet measured. The product's first move is to close that gap — and that move is the next experiment.

    15. One-screen recap¶

    Product. DriftDeck — ML observability that detects and prices model drift when labels are late, partial, or selective.

    Why it exists. Existing drift tools assume labels arrive with predictions; in production they don't (chargebacks take 30–90 days), and the loss is invisible until it is large.

    The wedge. Delayed-label-native monitoring — model the delay, give the earliest provable answer, price the wait.

    The engine. Hybrid detector: label-free early warning (fast, may be false) + delayed supervised confirmation (reliable, floor-bounded).

    The metric. Damage area → drift cost in dollars.

    The moat. Four lemmas (reaction floor, 1/√p, batch inflation, identifiability) that let the product state honest limits instead of marketing promises.

    The build order. Validate the lemmas (Exp-05) → MVP on fraud → harden → distribute.

    The evidence. Exp-01/02 prove the adaptation mechanism; the harness is the future backtest engine; the research roadmap is the product's phase A–B.
