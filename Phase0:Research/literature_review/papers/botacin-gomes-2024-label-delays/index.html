    Towards More Realistic Evaluations: The Impact of Label Delays in Malware Detection Pipelines¶

    Full citation: Botacin, M., & Gomes, H. (2024). Towards more realistic evaluations: The impact of label delays in malware detection pipelines. Computers & Security, 147 (per ScienceDirect pii S0167404824004279). Author-hosted PDF: https://marcusbotacin.github.io/files/label-delays.pdf · Code: https://github.com/marcusbotacin/ML.delay.experiments

    Status: ✅ Verified (full text read; COSE 2024 confirmed via GitHub + ScienceDirect)

    Why this paper matters¶

    This is the closest published empirical treatment of our exact Ch3 problem: it measures, with real malware data and real drift detectors (DDM/EDDM), what label delay does to a drift-retraining pipeline — and it proposes the closest existing thing to our hybrid (a pseudo-label secondary classifier as an early signal).

    What they did¶

    • Setting: malware detection modeled as a delayed and partially labelled evolving data stream (explicitly citing the Gomes 2022 survey).
    • Pipelines: Naive Triage (no retraining), Triage-Update (drift detector triggers retrain), Triage-Oracle-Update (sandbox = oracle), and Triage-Oracle-Eager-Update (secondary classifier produces pseudo-labels to bridge the delay before the oracle's ground truth arrives).
    • Data: DREBIN (Android malware, temporally ordered by first-seen), 250 epochs × 500 samples; replicated on Androzoo (~284k samples, ~270 epochs).
    • Detectors: DDM and EDDM, epoch-level drift checks.
    • Classifier: Random Forest, FPR < 1% enforced, 10 executions averaged.
    • New metric: the exposure metric — cumulative sum of False Negatives over samples and days: Exp(samples; days) = Σ_day Σ_sample FN(s, d). Non-decreasing; captures how long a user is exposed to a missed sample.

    Key numbers (all from the paper)¶

    Finding Number
    Drift mitigation reduces exposure ~50%
    Delayed labels increase exposure ~50% (up to 75% detection decrease)
    Delay at which exposure +50% vs immediate (DDM) 81 epochs (~⅓ of stream)
    Delay at which exposure +50% vs immediate (EDDM) 61 epochs
    Exposure increase per 10 epochs of delay ~50k points (DDM), ~60k (EDDM)
    Pseudo-labels reduce detection loss 30% loss instead of 70% loss
    10% queue capacity ≈ equivalent delay 100+ epochs (oracle-only)
    Queue capacity with same exposure using pseudo-labels down to 70% (vs 100%)
    Metrics comparison (Table 4) AUT-F1 0.52→0.18 as delay 0→80; Exposure 0.49→0.90

    Pseudo-label detail: with 21 epochs delay, mitigation strong; effect weakens to nil at 45 epochs, then reappears for larger delays (secondary model's own predictions become relevant again). Updating the secondary model on drift points matters (un-updated secondary degrades over time).

    How it differs from Ch3 / our lemmas¶

    • Empirical, not formal. It demonstrates the reaction floor (Lemma 1) but proves no bound, and does not touch power loss (Lemma 2), batch autocorrelation (Lemma 3), or identifiability (Lemma 4).
    • Application-specific. DREBIN/Androzoo + DDM/EDDM only; no synthetic ground-truth drift positions, so no clean detection-delay curve vs label latency.
    • Pseudo-labels, not a two-stage hybrid. Their eager-update secondary classifier is a label-free early signal, but there is no explicit confirmation protocol, no warning-within-tolerance-window machinery, and no calibration of false-alarm cost of unconfirmed warnings.
    • Their exposure metric is a strong candidate for how we should frame Ch2/Ch3 "reality tax" results (time-aware, cumulative) — and we must cite it when introducing any time-aware metric.

    What Ch3 must NOT claim as novel¶

    • "Delayed labels measurably degrade drift-based retraining" — already quantified here.
    • "A secondary/label-free signal mitigates delay" — already shown (pseudo-labels).
    • "You need time-aware metrics" — already argued (exposure vs AUT).

    What remains open (our space): formal reaction-floor bound; power-loss law vs label rate on detectors in isolation; false-alarm inflation from batch arrival; identifiability ceiling; a confirmation protocol (early warning → delayed supervised confirmation → calibrated action) evaluated against both naive delayed-supervised and label-free-only baselines.
